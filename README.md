Customer Behaviour and Churn Analysis Dashboard Report
Executive Summary

The Customer Behaviour and Churn Analysis Dashboard provides a comprehensive view of customer retention and churn patterns. The analysis reveals that while the business maintains a relatively strong retention rate of 83%, customer churn remains a significant challenge, with 948 customers lost out of 5,630 total customers, resulting in an overall churn rate of 17%.

The dashboard highlights clear relationships between customer complaints, payment methods, marital status, product preferences, and inactivity periods, offering valuable insights into customer behavior and potential retention opportunities.

Key Performance Overview
Metric	ValueTotal Customers	5,630
Total Churned Customers	948
Overall Churn Rate	17%
Retention Rate	83%
Complaint-Related Churn Rate	32%
Average Cashback (Retained Customers)	180.6
Interpretation

The organization has successfully retained the majority of its customers, with more than four out of five customers continuing to engage with the business. However, the loss of nearly one thousand customers suggests there are underlying issues affecting customer satisfaction and loyalty.

The complaint-related churn rate of 32% is particularly concerning, indicating that customer service experiences play a major role in customer retention.

Customer Profile Analysis
Retained vs. Churned Customers

The baseline comparison shows notable differences between retained and churned customers:

Retained customers significantly outnumber churned customers.
Retained customers generally exhibit longer tenure with the company.
Cashback amounts appear higher among retained customers.
Customer engagement, measured through order frequency and order values, appears stronger among retained customers.
Business Insight

Customers who receive greater value through loyalty benefits such as cashback and who have developed longer relationships with the company are less likely to churn. This suggests that loyalty and reward programs contribute positively to customer retention.

Impact of Customer Complaints

One of the most striking findings is the strong connection between complaints and customer churn.

Findings
Customers who have submitted complaints demonstrate significantly higher churn rates than those who have not.
The churn rate among complaining customers is nearly three times higher than non-complaining customers.
Business Interpretation

Customer complaints are acting as an early warning signal for churn risk. When issues are not resolved effectively, customers appear more likely to leave.

Recommendation
Implement faster complaint-resolution processes.
Track unresolved complaints closely.
Introduce proactive follow-up communication with dissatisfied customers.
Use complaint data as part of churn prediction models.

Customer Inactivity Analysis

The inactivity trend provides one of the clearest indicators of churn.

Key Observation

As the number of days since a customer's last order increases, churn rates rise sharply.

Business Interpretation

Customer inactivity is a strong predictor of future churn. Customers who stop purchasing for extended periods are significantly more likely to leave permanently.

Recommendation

Implement automated re-engagement strategies such as:

Personalized email campaigns
Discount vouchers
Reminder notifications
Loyalty point incentives
Product recommendation campaigns

The goal should be to reconnect with customers before inactivity reaches critical levels.

Overall Business Insights

The dashboard points to five major churn drivers:

1. Customer Complaints

Complaint-related customers are substantially more likely to leave.

2. Inactivity

Long gaps between purchases strongly correlate with churn.

3. Payment Preferences

Cash-on-Delivery users show elevated churn risk.

4. Customer Demographics

Single customers demonstrate the highest churn behavior.

5. Product Preferences

Mobile and fashion-focused customers are more likely to churn than customers purchasing essential or recurring items.

Strategic Recommendations

To reduce churn and improve customer retention, the organization should prioritize:

Immediate Actions

✅ Improve complaint resolution processes

✅ Monitor inactivity and create early intervention programs

✅ Enhance loyalty and cashback rewards

✅ Promote digital payment adoption

✅ Launch targeted retention campaigns for high-risk customer segments

Long-Term Initiatives

✅ Develop predictive churn models using complaint, inactivity, and transaction data

✅ Create category-specific retention strategies

✅ Strengthen customer loyalty programs

✅ Implement customer health scoring systems

✅ Improve personalization across all customer touchpoints

Conclusion

The dashboard shows that the company is performing well in retaining most of its customers, with an 83% retention rate. However, the 17% churn rate represents a meaningful revenue risk. The strongest indicators of churn are customer complaints, prolonged inactivity, certain payment methods, and specific customer demographics and purchasing categories.

By focusing on proactive customer engagement, faster issue resolution, and personalized retention strategies, the business can significantly reduce churn, increase customer lifetime value, and strengthen long-term customer relationships.
